# special-situations

General U.S. educational guidance: first protect near-term cash needs, legal/tax obligations, insurance, and financial resilience; only then select investments. Asset allocation should follow the date and importance of each goal, not merely age or stated risk preference. An investor may have high willingness to take risk but low capacity because of dependents, unstable income, debt, or a near-term obligation.  
Sources: [Investor.gov—Asset Allocation and Diversification](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/mutual-funds-etfs/asset-allocation-and-diversification), [Investor.gov—Risk Tolerance](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/role-risk)

## SS-001 — Dependents, children, and special-needs family members

### Priorities

- Maintain an emergency reserve sized to household obligations and income reliability before taking substantial market risk.
- Protect the income that supports dependents with appropriate health, disability, and—where others rely on the investor’s income—term life insurance.
- Review account beneficiaries, wills, powers of attorney, and guardianship instructions. Beneficiary designations on retirement accounts and insurance can supersede a will.
- Continue receiving an available employer retirement-plan match, but do not fund a child’s education at the expense of essential emergency savings or the caregiver’s retirement security.
- Match education money to its likely use date. A state-sponsored 529 plan can provide tax benefits for qualified education expenses, but investment choices should become more conservative as the distribution date approaches. A 529 is not automatically appropriate for every education goal.
- For a disabled dependent, investigate an ABLE account and/or a properly drafted special-needs trust before transferring assets directly to the person. Ownership can affect eligibility for means-tested benefits, and ABLE, SSI, and Medicaid rules can be technical and change over time.  
Sources: [IRS Publication 501—Dependents](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p501), [IRS—529 Plans](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/529-plans), [IRS—ABLE Accounts](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/529a-able-accounts), [SSA—ABLE Accounts](https://www.ssa.gov/ssi/spotlights/spot-able.html), [Medicaid—Special Needs Trusts](https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/eligibility/trusts/index.html)

### Risks

- A parent may overconcentrate in a volatile investment while believing it is “for the children.”
- Custodial accounts generally become the child’s property under the applicable state law when the child reaches the required age; they are not the same as a 529 or a trust.
- Direct gifts to a person receiving public benefits can disrupt eligibility.
- Life insurance, annuities, and “college investment” products may carry high costs or unsuitable guarantees. Examine ownership, fees, liquidity, and tax treatment rather than buying solely because an agent labels a product as family protection.
- Avoid promising that investment returns will cover future tuition or care costs.

### Professional help

Refer to an estate-planning or elder-law attorney for special-needs planning, guardianship, trusts, or substantial assets. A fee-only financial planner can coordinate insurance, education funding, and retirement goals; a tax professional should review gifts, trusts, and account ownership.

## SS-002 — Marriage, separation, and divorce

### Priorities

- Before combining finances, disclose assets, debts, income, account ownership, tax returns, insurance, stock compensation, and beneficiary designations.
- Agree on a household cash reserve, debt policy, account access, and investment objectives. Joint ownership is not required for every account.
- After marriage, review retirement-plan and insurance beneficiaries, wills, powers of attorney, and tax withholding. Federal retirement-plan spousal rights and state community-property rules can affect beneficiary changes.
- During separation, preserve statements and transaction records, avoid hiding or transferring assets, and do not liquidate jointly owned investments without legal advice.
- In divorce, distinguish marital, separate, and commingled property under state law. A qualified domestic relations order may be required to divide an employer retirement plan; an IRA transfer incident to divorce follows different procedures. A court order does not by itself resolve investment allocation, tax withholding, or beneficiary issues.
- Rebuild the post-divorce plan around the recipient’s own income, housing, insurance, emergency reserve, retirement needs, and tax filing status rather than simply dividing the existing portfolio.  
Sources: [IRS Publication 501—Filing Status and Dependents](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p501), [IRS Publication 504—Divorced or Separated Individuals](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p504), [U.S. Department of Labor—QDRO FAQs](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/faqs/qdro), [IRS—Retirement Topics: Divorce](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-divorce)

### Risks

- Leaving a former spouse named on a retirement account or insurance policy can produce an unintended result, though federal and state rules differ.
- A settlement based on account balances can ignore embedded capital-gains tax, penalties, liquidity, restricted stock, pensions, or debt.
- Taking a taxable retirement distribution to fund a settlement may create tax and penalty consequences.
- Investment advice should not be used to decide legal rights, support obligations, or whether to sign a settlement.

### Professional help

A family-law attorney should review property division and any QDRO. A CPA or enrolled agent should model filing status, support payments, stock compensation, and taxable transfers. A CFP® professional or other fee-only planner can prepare a post-settlement cash-flow and investment plan.

## SS-003 — Windfalls, inheritances, and sudden wealth

### Priorities

- Secure the money first: verify the source, move cash to appropriately protected institutions, improve account security, and pause irreversible purchases or investments while facts are confirmed.
- Inventory the assets and determine whether the transfer is cash, taxable securities, real estate, a business interest, a trust distribution, or an inherited retirement account.
- Preserve statements, the decedent’s date-of-death valuation, cost-basis records, trust documents, and distribution notices.
- Set aside money for known taxes, debts, charitable commitments, and near-term spending before investing the remainder.
- Create a written policy for spending, gifting, investing, and family requests. A diversified, low-cost portfolio is usually easier to monitor than a collection of private deals, concentrated securities, and complex products.
- Inherited property generally receives a basis determined under estate-tax rules, commonly related to fair market value at death, but exceptions and valuation issues exist. Inherited retirement accounts have beneficiary-specific distribution and tax rules; do not assume that every beneficiary can simply withdraw whenever convenient or defer indefinitely.
- Treat unsolicited inheritance notices, requests for “release fees,” guaranteed returns, urgent wire instructions, and requests to send crypto as potential fraud. Verify independently using contact information you locate yourself.  
Sources: [IRS Publication 551—Basis of Assets](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p551), [IRS Publication 559—Survivors, Executors, and Administrators](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p559), [IRS Publication 590-B—Distributions from Individual Retirement Arrangements](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590b), [Investor.gov—Avoid Investment Fraud](https://www.investor.gov/protect-your-investments/fraud/types-fraud), [FINRA—Sudden Wealth](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/sudden-wealth)

### Risks

- A large balance can create overconfidence, family pressure, concentrated bets, lifestyle inflation, and exposure to unregistered offerings.
- Selling inherited assets, distributing from an inherited IRA, or transferring assets to relatives can have different tax consequences.
- An investor should not make major gifts or disclaim an inheritance until tax, estate, and benefits consequences are understood.

### Professional help

Use an estate attorney and tax professional for trusts, inherited retirement accounts, valuation, gifting, estate tax, or cross-border inheritance. A fee-only planner can provide a second opinion on allocation and spending without taking custody of assets.

## SS-004 — Imminent home purchase

### Priorities

- Define the purchase horizon and the full cash target: down payment, closing and moving costs, inspections, repairs, reserves, and ongoing housing costs.
- Keep money needed for a near-term purchase in liquid, low-volatility vehicles such as an appropriately insured deposit account, Treasury securities matched to the time horizon, or a suitable government money-market option. Do not rely on equities, crypto, or concentrated stock to produce the required down payment.
- Preserve a separate emergency reserve after closing. A buyer who uses every dollar for the down payment may be vulnerable to job loss, repairs, insurance increases, or a rate reset.
- Compare the proposed mortgage payment with realistic taxes, insurance, maintenance, HOA costs, and other debt obligations. Buying a home is not automatically a superior investment; it creates geographic and financial concentration.
- Avoid withdrawing retirement money unless the tax, penalty, opportunity-cost, and repayment implications have been carefully evaluated.
- Use a HUD-approved housing counselor for budgeting, assistance programs, or first-time-buyer questions.  
Sources: [CFPB—Buying a House](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/), [HUD—Housing Counseling](https://www.hud.gov/counseling), [Investor.gov—Asset Allocation and Diversification](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/mutual-funds-etfs/asset-allocation-and-diversification), [TreasuryDirect—Treasury Bills](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/marketable-securities/treasury-bills/)

### Risks

- A market decline immediately before closing can delay or eliminate the purchase.
- A low down payment may increase borrowing costs or require mortgage insurance; a high down payment may leave inadequate liquidity.
- Home equity is illiquid and cannot reliably substitute for an emergency fund.
- Do not recommend a specific mortgage, lender, or affordability amount without current income, debt, rate, and cash-flow information.

### Professional help

Refer to a housing counselor or mortgage professional for loan comparisons and affordability. Use a tax professional for home-sale, rental, or self-employment issues and an attorney for unusual ownership structures.

## SS-005 — Job loss, layoff, or sharply reduced income

### Priorities

- Stop or reduce optional investing temporarily if necessary and preserve cash for housing, food, health care, transportation, insurance, and minimum debt payments.
- Determine severance, unemployment benefits, unused leave, health-insurance continuation, and retirement-plan options. Losing employer coverage may trigger a special enrollment opportunity for Marketplace coverage.
- Reassess the emergency reserve based on the likelihood of reemployment, household dependents, industry, and access to other income—not just a generic month target.
- Avoid panic-selling a long-term diversified portfolio solely because markets are falling, but do not maintain an aggressive allocation if the money is now needed for living expenses.
- Treat employer stock and unvested or recently vested compensation separately from the core portfolio. Job loss and a decline in the employer’s stock can occur together.
- Consider retirement withdrawals only after reviewing alternatives, taxes, penalties, creditor protections, and the effect on future retirement income.  
Sources: [HealthCare.gov—Special Enrollment After Job Loss](https://www.healthcare.gov/ losing-job-based-coverage/), [U.S. Department of Labor—COBRA](https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/health-plans/cobra), [Investor.gov—Saving and Investing](https://www.investor.gov/financial-tools-calculators/calculators/savings-goal-calculator), [IRS—Retirement Plan Distributions](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-tax-on-early-distributions)

### Risks

- Selling depressed assets, taking high-cost loans, or cashing out retirement accounts can convert a temporary income shock into a permanent loss.
- Borrowing against a retirement plan may become due after separation from service and can create tax consequences if not handled correctly.
- Job loss may also remove life, disability, and health coverage; the investment plan cannot compensate for an uninsured income risk.

### Professional help

A nonprofit credit counselor can help with debt triage. A tax professional should review severance, equity compensation, unemployment benefits, and retirement distributions. Seek legal help for severance agreements, bankruptcy, or creditor issues.

## SS-006 — Caring for parents or other adults

### Priorities

- Separate the caregiver’s assets and retirement plan from the parent’s assets and obligations. Set a sustainable monthly caregiving budget and agree among family members who pays for what.
- Obtain proper legal authority—such as a durable financial power of attorney or health-care authorization—before managing another adult’s accounts. Do not add a caregiver as a joint owner merely for convenience without understanding the ownership, gift, creditor, and inheritance consequences.
- Locate the parent’s insurance, benefits, pension, Social Security, debts, accounts, estate documents, and long-term-care preferences.
- Preserve the caregiver’s emergency fund, retirement contributions, and insurance where possible; sacrificing the caregiver’s retirement can shift the burden to the next generation.
- Understand that Medicare generally does not function as comprehensive long-term-care insurance. Medicaid eligibility, spend-down rules, estate recovery, home ownership, and transfers are state- and fact-specific.
- Use trusted contacts, account alerts, limited access, and documentation to reduce elder financial exploitation.  
Sources: [Administration for Community Living—Eldercare Locator](https://eldercare.acl.gov/), [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau—Managing Someone Else’s Money](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/managing-someone-elses-money/), [Medicare—Long-Term Care](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/long-term-care), [Medicaid—Estate Recovery](https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/eligibility/estate-recovery/index.html), [FINRA—Trusted Contact Person](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/trusted-contact-person)

### Risks

- Co-signing, commingling, informal loans, or selling a parent’s investments without authority can create legal, tax, and family disputes.
- A parent may be targeted by annuity, crypto, romance, or “investment opportunity” scams.
- Caregiving costs and reduced work hours can make the caregiver’s own risk capacity much lower than it appears from account balances.

### Professional help

Refer to an elder-law attorney for powers of attorney, Medicaid planning, trusts, and capacity issues. A benefits counselor, CPA, and fee-only planner can coordinate cash flow and long-term-care decisions.

## SS-007 — International students, foreign nationals, and visa holders

### Priorities

- Determine U.S. tax status for the relevant year before choosing accounts. Immigration status and tax residency are not identical; students and other visa holders may be subject to special substantial-presence counting rules and exemptions.
- Confirm whether the person has U.S. compensation and is eligible to contribute to an IRA or employer plan. Do not infer eligibility from having a brokerage account, a Social Security number, or a visa.
- Complete the correct broker tax forms, such as Form W-8BEN where applicable, and understand withholding and reporting for dividends, interest, and sales.
- Ask whether the person has foreign brokerage accounts, bank accounts, foreign mutual funds, trusts, or assets. FBAR, Form 8938, PFIC, foreign-trust, and treaty rules can apply; foreign mutual funds can be especially complex for U.S. taxpayers.
- Use broadly diversified investments with portability and low administrative complexity if the person may leave the United States. Confirm that the receiving country permits the account and investment.
- Keep immigration and tax advice separate. Passive ownership of securities is different from operating a business or performing unauthorized employment, but visa-specific facts matter.  
Sources: [IRS—Taxation of Nonresident Aliens](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/taxation-of-nonresident-aliens), [IRS—Foreign Students, Scholars, Teachers, and Researchers](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-students-scholars-teachers-researchers-and-exchange-visitors), [IRS—Substantial Presence Test](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/substantial-presence-test), [IRS—FBAR](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/report-of-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts-fbar), [IRS—Form 8938](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8938), [IRS—Form 8621 and PFICs](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8621), [USCIS—Employment](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-2-part-f)

### Risks

- A U.S. brokerage account does not eliminate foreign-country tax, reporting, currency, estate, or account-transfer issues.
- U.S. tax treatment of dividends and capital gains may differ from the person’s home-country treatment.
- A visa holder could unintentionally create employment or business activity that conflicts with status.
- Avoid recommending a “backdoor” account strategy or foreign fund without reviewing all tax forms and the person’s countries of tax residence.

### Professional help

Use a cross-border CPA or enrolled agent for tax residency, treaty, PFIC, FBAR, Form 8938, and retirement-account questions. Refer visa or employment-status questions to a qualified immigration attorney.

## SS-008 — Crypto, options, leveraged products, and other speculative assets

### Priorities

- Fund emergency needs, high-priority debt repayment, insurance, and diversified core investments before considering speculation.
- Use only money the investor can lose without affecting housing, education, dependents, retirement, or taxes. Treat the speculative holding as a separate, capped risk budget rather than as the core portfolio.
- Avoid borrowing, margin, leveraged tokens, complex options strategies, or pledging essential assets to speculate. Losses can exceed the initial cash in some leveraged arrangements.
- Verify custody, withdrawal rules, fees, conflicts, cybersecurity, bankruptcy treatment, and whether any protection applies. Do not assume FDIC or SIPC protection for crypto assets.
- Maintain transaction-level records for purchases, sales, exchanges, staking, lending, mining, gifts, and transfers. Digital-asset tax treatment depends on the transaction and basis.
- Be particularly skeptical of guaranteed returns, social-media tips, celebrity endorsements, “zero-risk” yield, private keys or seed phrases requested by another person, and urgent transfers.  
Sources: [FINRA—Crypto Assets](https://www.finra.org/investors/investing/investment-products/crypto-assets), [SEC—Crypto Asset Securities Investor Bulletin](https://www.sec.gov/oiea/investor-alerts-and-bulletins/ib_cryptoassets), [Investor.gov—Crypto Investment Frauds](https://www.investor.gov/investment-scams/crypto-investment-scams), [IRS—Digital Assets](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/digital-assets), [Investor.gov—Margin Accounts](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/margin), [FINRA—Options](https://www.finra.org/investors/investing/investment-products/options)

### Risks

- Extreme volatility, permanent loss, fraud, hacking, operational failure, illiquidity, regulatory change, tax errors, and concentration can occur together.
- A crypto exchange, token, private placement, mining operation, NFT, meme stock, or leveraged ETF is not interchangeable with a diversified index fund.
- “Diversification” within several correlated speculative tokens may provide little actual diversification.

### Professional help

Refer to a tax professional for substantial or complex digital-asset activity and to a registered investment professional for portfolio-concentration or derivatives questions. Legal help may be needed for custody, bankruptcy, fraud, or business structures.

## SS-009 — ESG, sustainable, faith-based, or other values preferences

### Priorities

- Clarify the value constraint: exclusions, positive-impact goals, shareholder engagement, climate transition, labor practices, faith screens, local investment, or a combination.
- Decide what tradeoff is acceptable among diversification, tracking error, fees, tax efficiency, liquidity, and expected risk. Values preferences do not remove ordinary investment risk.
- Read the prospectus, methodology, holdings, voting policy, benchmark, turnover, and expenses. Do not rely on a fund name, rating, marketing label, or a single ESG score.
- Check whether the screening rule applies to all holdings, how revenue thresholds are defined, how controversial issuers are treated, and how often the portfolio changes.
- Document the preference in an investment policy statement and review it periodically as products and personal priorities change.
- Consider low-cost diversified funds first; use direct ownership or shareholder engagement only when the investor understands the added research, concentration, and voting burden.  
Sources: [Investor.gov—Sustainable Investing](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/sustainable), [SEC—Investment Company Names Rule](https://www.sec.gov/rules/final/2023/33-11238.pdf), [SEC—Investor Bulletin: Environmental, Social, and Governance Funds](https://www.sec.gov/oiea/investor-alerts-and-bulletins/investor-bulletin-environmental-social-and-governance-esg-funds)

### Risks

- Screening can reduce diversification, increase fees or turnover, and create sector or geographic concentration.
- ESG labels and ratings may use different definitions; two funds with the same label may hold very different securities.
- A values-based portfolio can underperform or outperform a broad benchmark for extended periods. That is not, by itself, evidence that the values screen is defective.
- Avoid claiming that an investment is impact-positive or “green” without examining methodology and holdings.

### Professional help

A fee-only planner can test whether exclusions undermine the investor’s required diversification. A tax professional can evaluate taxable sales, tax-loss harvesting, and concentrated appreciated holdings.

## SS-010 — Very low net worth or financial fragility

### Priorities

- Focus first on stable cash flow, essential insurance, an emergency reserve, and repayment or restructuring of high-cost debt.
- Capture an available employer retirement match when doing so does not cause missed essentials, but do not invest emergency money in volatile assets.
- Use low-cost, no-minimum diversified funds or a workplace plan where suitable. Avoid account fees, unnecessary trading, payday loans, margin, and products with surrender charges.
- If the person has earned income and a low current tax rate, a Roth account may be worth considering, but eligibility, contribution limits, tax credits, and liquidity rules must be checked for the applicable year.
- Use nonprofit credit counseling, benefits programs, and employer resources when cash flow is negative. An investment portfolio is not a substitute for solving unaffordable debt or housing costs.
- Keep the plan simple: automatic contributions, broad diversification, and a written emergency-first rule are generally more useful than security selection.

### Risks

- A small balance does not justify high-risk bets; a loss may delay rent, tuition, transportation, or debt repayment.
- Minimum-balance fees, trading spreads, cash-advance costs, and “free” high-turnover products can materially reduce results.
- The person may have low risk capacity even if they describe themselves as risk tolerant.

### Professional help

Refer to a nonprofit credit counselor or benefits counselor for financial distress. A low-cost or pro bono planner may help with account selection and benefits; do not refer a fragile client first to a commission-driven product seller.

Sources: [CFPB—Credit Counseling](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-credit-counseling-en-1451/), [Investor.gov—Fees and Expenses](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/getting-started/why-you-should-pay-attention-fees), [IRS—Retirement Savings Contributions Credit](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-savings-contributions-savers-credit)

## SS-011 — Very high net worth, business owners, and concentrated wealth

### Priorities

- Map the entire balance sheet, including private businesses, real estate, employer stock, carried interests, trusts, insurance, art, crypto, and private funds. Net worth is not the same as liquid investable wealth.
- Establish a liquidity reserve for taxes, spending, capital calls, business obligations, charitable commitments, and estate expenses.
- Reduce uncompensated concentration gradually, considering tax basis, voting control, securities law, charitable gifts, hedging constraints, and the investor’s tolerance for losing control.
- Coordinate taxable, tax-deferred, and tax-free accounts; consider asset location, charitable giving, Roth-conversion analysis, required distributions, and tax-loss harvesting only after modeling tax effects.
- Review estate documents, successor ownership, business-continuity planning, gifting, trusts, beneficiary designations, and estate-tax liquidity with qualified professionals.
- Treat private equity, hedge funds, venture investments, nontraded REITs, structured notes, direct lending, and other alternatives as complex, illiquid, fee-heavy exposures—not as automatic diversification. Review valuation methods, lockups, redemption gates, leverage, capital calls, and total fees.
- Use an investment policy statement and require independent custody, transparent reporting, written fee disclosures, and conflict disclosures.

### Risks

- A profitable business or appreciated stock can make the investor appear diversified by asset value while remaining highly exposed to one company, industry, customer, location, or tax regime.
- Private investments may be difficult to value or sell, and “accredited investor” status does not make an investment safe.
- Complex products can obscure leverage, embedded fees, counterparty risk, and tax consequences.
- Wealth can attract fraud, undue influence, family conflict, and advisers whose compensation is not aligned with the client’s interests.

### Professional help

A coordinated team may include a fee-only fiduciary planner, CPA or tax attorney, estate attorney, business attorney, insurance specialist, and—when appropriate—an independent investment manager. Verify advisers through [SEC IAPD](https://adviserinfo.sec.gov/), [FINRA BrokerCheck](https://brokercheck.finra.org/), and disclosed Form ADV information. Ask exactly how each professional is paid, what conflicts exist, whether the person acts as a fiduciary, and who has custody of assets.

Sources: [SEC—Investor Adviser Public Disclosure](https://adviserinfo.sec.gov/), [FINRA—BrokerCheck](https://www.finra.org/investors/learn-to-invest/choosing-investment-professional), [SEC—Private Funds](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/private-funds), [Investor.gov—Fees and Expenses](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/getting-started/why-you-should-pay-attention-fees)

## Cross-situation decision rules

- **If money has a fixed, near-term use or supports essential living costs,** prioritize liquidity and principal stability over expected long-run return.
- **If another person depends on the investor’s income or assets,** review insurance, beneficiaries, estate documents, and account ownership before increasing investment risk.
- **If a legal relationship, tax residency, inheritance, trust, or retirement beneficiary has changed,** pause irreversible transfers and obtain legal or tax review.
- **If one employer, business, property, country, sector, or speculative asset dominates wealth or income,** assess total exposure before adding more.
- **If the investor cannot explain an investment’s fees, liquidity, tax treatment, custody, and maximum loss,** do not recommend it as a core holding.
- **If the situation involves divorce, special-needs planning, elder care, immigration status, substantial inheritance, complex digital assets, business ownership, or estate-tax exposure,** general educational guidance is insufficient; refer to the appropriate qualified professional.
