# ai-guardrails — US consumer AI investing app

**Research note; US scope.** This is a product/compliance framework, not legal advice. The app's legal classification depends on its actual features, compensation, communications, execution/custody arrangements, and state law—not on a label such as “education only” or on a disclaimer. Obtain securities counsel before launch and whenever features change.

## 1. Regulatory boundary: education, recommendation, adviser, broker

### General education is the safer boundary

The app may provide genuinely general information—risk/return concepts, diversification, historical asset-class information, definitions, retirement-planning concepts, descriptions of employer-plan options, and clearly labeled hypothetical examples—without selecting a security or telling a person what to buy, sell, or hold. FINRA’s suitability guidance recognizes general financial and investment information and certain theory-based asset-allocation models as outside Rule 2111 when they do not, alone or together, recommend particular securities and when required assumptions and material facts are disclosed.

A user-specific output can still be a recommendation even if phrased as “consider,” “you may want to,” “your best option,” or generated by an algorithm. The analysis must consider the whole experience: onboarding questions, model portfolios, defaults, rankings, alerts, nudges, rebalancing, “buy” buttons, watchlists, and recommendations to hold or roll over an account.

### Likely Investment Advisers Act exposure

The Investment Advisers Act generally covers a person or firm that, for compensation, is in the business of providing advice about securities. Compensation can be direct or indirect; advertising, referral payments, spread/revenue sharing, subscription fees, and monetization of the advisory service can matter. A personalized model portfolio, security recommendation, allocation instruction, or automated management service may therefore be investment-adviser activity even when no human speaks to the user.

If the app is an investment adviser, it must address registration or a valid exemption at the federal or state level, fiduciary duties, disclosures, conflicts, books and records, privacy/cybersecurity, compliance procedures, supervision, and any custody, advertising, solicitation, or performance-marketing requirements. A disclaimer cannot turn a recommendation into education or waive an adviser’s fiduciary duty. Form ADV and, where applicable, Form CRS and the adviser brochure must be accurate, current, and delivered as required.

The SEC’s internet-adviser exemption has specific conditions, including an operational interactive website and advice delivered exclusively through that website. Do not rely on an “internet app” label; confirm the current rule, amendments, and state requirements with counsel.

### Broker-dealer and FINRA boundary

FINRA Rule 2111 applies to FINRA member firms and associated persons—not to every standalone software company. It requires reasonable-basis, customer-specific, and quantitative suitability for a recommendation involving securities. The customer profile includes age, other investments, financial situation and needs, tax status, objectives, experience, time horizon, liquidity needs, risk tolerance, and other disclosed information. The rule expressly reaches investment strategies and explicit recommendations to hold. A disclaimer cannot disclaim Rule 2111 responsibilities.

If the app is operated by, embedded in, or used by a broker-dealer or associated person, it must be integrated into the firm’s suitability, supervision, communications, recordkeeping, and complaint controls. FINRA Rules 2010, 2111, 2210, and 3110 are especially relevant, along with applicable account-opening, communications, and product-specific rules.

### Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI)

Reg BI applies to a broker-dealer or associated person making a recommendation to a retail customer involving a security or investment strategy, including an account recommendation. It is not the general rule for an independent investment adviser, whose Advisers Act fiduciary duty applies instead. Reg BI requires all four components:

- **Disclosure:** before or at the recommendation, disclose material facts about the relationship, capacity, scope, fees, costs, services, and material conflicts.
- **Care:** understand risks, rewards, and costs; have a reasonable basis for the recommendation; consider reasonably available alternatives; and consider the retail customer’s profile, including potential costs and conflicts.
- **Conflict:** identify, mitigate, or eliminate conflicts as required by the rule, particularly incentives to put the firm’s or representative’s interest ahead of the customer’s.
- **Compliance:** maintain policies and procedures reasonably designed to achieve compliance.

Reg BI is not satisfied by a generic “not advice” banner. If the app is not a broker-dealer, Reg BI may not directly govern it, but its care, conflict, and disclosure principles are a useful minimum design standard and do not replace an Advisers Act analysis.

**Primary sources:** SEC robo-adviser guidance, SEC Reg BI materials, Investment Advisers Act materials, FINRA Rule 2111 and FAQ (links below).

## 2. Required product posture

### A. Decide and document the intended service

Before implementation, classify each feature as:

1. **Education:** no individualized security recommendation, no transaction prompt, no ranking that reasonably functions as a recommendation.
2. **Interactive planning:** user-specific projections or allocations that may still become advice depending on specificity, compensation, and presentation.
3. **Advice:** individualized securities advice, model portfolio, security selection, or ongoing monitoring/rebalancing.
4. **Execution/custody:** brokerage account, trade routing, discretionary authority, or possession/control of assets.

Maintain a feature-by-feature legal memo and change-control review. If the product intentionally provides personalized securities advice, design and register/operate it as a regulated service rather than trying to disclaim it away.

### B. Ask for the information needed to support personalization

Before a specific allocation or security recommendation, collect and timestamp, at minimum:

- age and household status;
- income, income stability, occupation, tax residence, and likely changes;
- cash, emergency reserves, debts, interest rates, payment obligations, and liquidity constraints;
- account types, employer match, pensions, insurance, other investments, concentrated positions, and restricted securities;
- goal, amount, priority, and date for each goal;
- time horizon and whether the money must be available before that horizon;
- risk capacity (ability to withstand loss without impairing a goal) separately from risk tolerance (willingness to withstand loss);
- investment experience, knowledge, and ability to understand the product;
- dependents, health/care obligations, legal or marital events, and relevant benefits;
- tax bracket or a clearly labeled estimate, tax-loss/capital-gain constraints, and values/preferences;
- whether the user wants an explanation, a general model, or an actionable recommendation.

Ask follow-up questions when an answer is inconsistent, stale, extreme, or material to the proposed action. Permit “unknown” and do not silently substitute an optimistic assumption. Keep a dated record of answers, assumptions, model version, data used, output, and user confirmations. Re-underwrite the profile after a material life event, at a suitable periodic review, and before a material change in allocation or product.

**Abstention rule:** if a material profile field is missing—especially horizon, liquidity need, debt/financial distress, tax status, risk capacity, or existing concentration—do not produce a specific security, trade, leverage, options, crypto, or allocation instruction. Ask for the missing facts or give general education with a visible “not enough information” explanation.

### C. Keep personalization bounded

The app may translate known facts into educational explanations, scenario comparisons, and a cautiously labeled diversified model only when the necessary facts, assumptions, and limitations are visible. It must not:

- infer risk tolerance merely from age, income, a quiz score, or prior clicks;
- treat a high salary as high risk capacity when debt, dependents, job concentration, or near-term goals reduce capacity;
- recommend an investment solely because it is popular, sponsored, proprietary, high-yielding, high-scoring, or profitable to the app;
- promote a single stock, sector, cryptoasset, options strategy, margin, leverage, private placement, or concentrated employer holding without enhanced risk review and appropriate human/regulatory controls;
- present a model allocation as a guarantee, prediction, individualized fiduciary plan, or tax result;
- tell a user to roll over, liquidate, borrow, exercise, convert, or trade without considering fees, tax, liquidity, alternatives, and the user’s full circumstances;
- use a disclaimer to cure an otherwise unsuitable recommendation.

For a general-education product, prefer categories, diversified funds as examples, and “questions to ask a qualified professional.” For a regulated advice product, implement a documented suitability/best-interest process, not merely a conversational prompt.

## 3. Disclosures and response language

### Base educational disclosure

Place this before actionable-looking content, not only in terms of service:

> **General educational information—not financial advice.** This app provides general information for US users and does not know all facts relevant to your situation. It is not investment, fiduciary, brokerage, legal, tax, accounting, insurance, or other professional advice, and no adviser-client relationship is created by using it. Do not rely on this response as a recommendation to buy, sell, hold, roll over, borrow, or use any security or strategy. Investments can lose value, including principal; past performance is not a guarantee of future results. Consider your goals, time horizon, liquidity, taxes, costs, and risk, verify all information, and consult an appropriately licensed human professional before acting.

Add, when relevant: “Quotes and other market data can be delayed, incomplete, stale, or wrong; they are not an offer or execution price. Check the source and timestamp.” Add a conflict statement identifying compensation, affiliates, sponsored placement, proprietary products, data use, and referral payments. Disclose that AI can be wrong and explain how to report an error.

This wording is suitable only for a product that actually stays within education. It does not eliminate adviser, broker-dealer, FINRA, Reg BI, state, or consumer-protection obligations if the product’s conduct is advisory or transactional.

### Response format for a permitted educational answer

1. State the user facts actually used and the material facts missing.
2. State the educational objective and distinguish risk tolerance from risk capacity.
3. Explain alternatives, costs, liquidity, tax, and downside risks in plain language.
4. Use a range or scenario rather than false precision; label every assumption.
5. Prefer diversified, low-cost building blocks before individual securities.
6. Cite the source and data timestamp for every dynamic fact.
7. State what the app cannot determine and give a next step or human referral.
8. Never use “guaranteed,” “risk-free,” “can’t lose,” “best for you,” “will outperform,” or certainty about a future price or return.

### If operating as a regulated adviser or broker-dealer

Give the legally required disclosures at the correct time and in the correct form; identify capacity; explain fees and conflicts in concrete dollars or examples where required; disclose model assumptions, limitations, rebalancing, monitoring, tax treatment, and material restrictions; retain records; and make public communications fair, balanced, and not misleading. Do not advertise the system as objective, unbiased, or fiduciary unless that claim is accurate for the service and its conflicts are addressed. Do not use testimonials, endorsements, hypothetical performance, or performance claims without complying with the applicable adviser marketing or broker-dealer communication rules.

## 4. Source and data-time requirements

Every answer must separate:

- **Stable facts:** law, definitions, fund prospectus facts, and historical information, with source publication date and retrieval date.
- **Dynamic facts:** price, yield, P/E, beta, assets, holdings, expense ratio, distributions, rates, limits, and tax figures, with source, exact as-of timestamp, timezone, market status, and whether real-time, delayed, or end-of-day.

For a requested ticker or fund, call the approved market-data/MCP tool rather than relying on model memory. Record provider, instrument identifier, exchange, currency, retrieval timestamp, data timestamp, field definitions, and any stale/error flag. For filings and tax/regulatory numbers, prefer the SEC, IRS, or issuer’s current primary document and state the relevant year. Do not combine fields from incompatible dates without saying so. A price is informational, not an execution quote; explain that spreads, fees, market movement, and order type affect execution.

If the tool is unavailable, the identifier is ambiguous, the field is stale, or sources disagree, say so and stop short of a current-price, return, valuation, or trade claim. Never fill a blank with a remembered or plausible value. Numbers such as annual limits, tax brackets, yields, returns, and prices require a source URL and an applicable year/date.

## 5. Anti-hallucination controls for tickers, funds, and prices

Implement controls outside the language model:

- Resolve a ticker against a canonical instrument database using ticker plus issuer, exchange, security type, and currency; display the legal name and identifier.
- Reject unknown, ambiguous, delisted, non-US, or similarly named instruments rather than guessing. Ask the user to clarify.
- Require a successful tool response for every displayed current price, return, ratio, holding, yield, or expense ratio; the model may not manufacture a response object.
- Validate units, decimal ranges, dates, currency, split adjustments, negative/undefined ratios, and fund share-class differences.
- Cross-check material facts against a second authoritative source or the issuer prospectus/SEC filing where practical; surface disagreement.
- Never invent a ticker, price, historical return, dividend, analyst target, or “live” value. Do not imply a quote is live unless the provider says it is.
- Separate fact from interpretation: “the provider reports X as of [timestamp]” is not “X is cheap” or “X will rise.”
- Prefer a diversified fund comparison and risk explanation before a single-stock result; show holdings overlap and concentration where data supports it.
- Add a visible “data may be delayed or inaccurate—verify before acting” control and a user report/correction path.

## 6. Distress, vulnerability, and high-risk situations

Use a supportive, nonjudgmental response when a user describes inability to pay essentials, eviction or utility shutoff, coercion, gambling losses, predatory lending, fraud, job loss, bankruptcy, severe anxiety, or self-harm. Do not suggest investing spare money, leverage, options, crypto, “make it back” trades, or a high-return rescue plan. Pause market recommendations and prioritize food, housing, utilities, medical care, minimum debt obligations, fraud containment, emergency cash, and benefits or creditor hardship programs. Do not tell a user to liquidate retirement assets or miss payments without appropriate professional review.

If the user may be in immediate danger or mentions suicide/self-harm, encourage immediate emergency help; in the US they can call or text **988** for the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, or call **911** for an emergency. The app is not a crisis counselor and should not promise confidentiality beyond its actual policy.

For financial distress, provide neutral links to the CFPB, HUD housing counseling where relevant, the user’s loan servicer or official student-loan resources, and reputable nonprofit credit counseling; warn against debt-relief firms demanding upfront fees or guaranteeing results. Ask permission before a referral, disclose any commercial relationship, and never rank a paid lead as a fiduciary recommendation.

Add heightened review or refuse automated action when the user is a minor, cognitively impaired, pressured by another person, a scam victim, in a divorce/estate dispute, unable to explain the product, or seeking a transaction with irreversible tax/legal consequences.

## 7. Human-expert escalation matrix

- **Fee-only fiduciary financial planner / CFP® professional:** personalized allocation, retirement-income or sequence-of-returns planning, large or concentrated holdings, stock-compensation decisions, pension/benefit coordination, a rollover, inheritance/windfall, incapacity, multiple conflicting goals, or a user who wants an ongoing fiduciary relationship. “Fee-only” must be verified; CFP Board’s certification and public disciplinary information should be checked, and the adviser’s actual services, compensation, and fiduciary scope confirmed in writing.
- **CPA or enrolled agent/tax attorney:** Roth conversions, capital gains/loss harvesting, RMDs, RSUs/ESPPs, option exercise, estimated payments, crypto reporting, business/self-employed plans, multi-state or nonresident issues, inheritance, and any answer depending on a user’s tax return. The app must not calculate a filing result from incomplete data or present tax education as tax advice.
- **Attorney:** divorce and property division, estate/trust/beneficiary issues, guardianship or incapacity, bankruptcy, securities or employment disputes, fraud recovery, contracts, immigration/cross-border consequences, or a contested beneficiary/title question.
- **Insurance or benefits professional:** disability, long-term-care, life-insurance needs, pension election, annuity, or employer-benefit decisions where coverage terms drive the plan. Disclose licensing and compensation.
- **Nonprofit credit counselor / housing counselor / bankruptcy attorney:** unmanageable unsecured debt, foreclosure/eviction risk, collections, debt settlement, or bankruptcy. Do not give individualized legal advice or promise that a program will reduce debt.
- **Student-loan specialist or official servicer resources:** PSLF, IDR, consolidation, forgiveness, co-signer, delinquency, or a repayment choice that materially changes an investment plan. Verify against current government and servicer information.
- **Human licensed investment professional and counsel:** any proposed trade involving leverage, options, margin, private securities, complex products, concentrated employer stock, or a recommendation the model cannot support from a complete profile and verified data.

A referral is not a loophole: the app must explain why it is escalating, provide how to verify registration (SEC IAPD for advisers and FINRA BrokerCheck for broker-dealer professionals), disclose conflicts, and avoid steering to a paid affiliate unless the relationship and compensation are clear.

## 8. Governance and operational safeguards

Maintain model and prompt versioning, source allowlists, retrieval logs, data timestamps, profile and recommendation records, adverse-event and hallucination reports, red-team tests, bias and vulnerability tests, approval for new products, rollback/kill switches, access controls, encryption, retention/deletion procedures, and a human complaint/escalation queue. Test contradictory profiles, stale data, ticker ambiguity, market closures, prompt injection, fabricated citations, distress language, minors, and requests to evade disclosure or suitability requirements.

If the service is regulated, map controls to the adviser compliance-program rule, books-and-records requirements, applicable privacy/cybersecurity obligations, FINRA supervision and communications rules, Reg BI policies where applicable, and state requirements. A human review queue must be able to stop a recommendation or trade; do not let the model silently retry with invented data.
